# AURA: submission

Name: AURA
Tagline: You have zero aura. Fix that, one beat at a time, while a crowd screams.

Team: Itchy & Scratchy (Dylan Merigaud, captain, and Dorian Poupard)
Track: Open Innovation Track
Partner technologies used: Google DeepMind (Gemini 3.8 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Flash Image "Nano Banana 2", Lyria 3.5 and Lyria 3 clip), Gradium (text to speech)

Short description: An aura battle in the browser. Five opponents from a nightclub to the Voodoo stage, arrow prompts on the beat of a generated funk track, a crowd screaming, and an announcer's verdict at the end, written by Gemini and read by Gradium.

## What it does

You are a nobody with zero aura, standing in a ring in a nightclub, and a DJ is telling you so. The camera sits over your shoulder and a crowd rings the fight. Arrow prompts, a two key mash and a hold arrive on the beat of a Brazilian funk track. Land them and the aura bar slides toward you, miss and it slides toward him, press the wrong arrow and it counts as cringe. Mashing charges a burst that you release on the drop, the 69. Land perfects and the song speeds up a little, miss and it slows down. Five fights take you from the club to a boss on the Voodoo stage, who has a second phase with a handheld camera and a denser chart. Each fight is about 40 seconds and ends with the announcer's verdict, a line Gemini wrote and Gradium reads.

## How I built it

The game reads the song, and the models never sit in the frame loop. Two layers.

Generation, at build time. `gen-cast` asks Gemini 3.8 Flash for each opponent's story, taunts and announcer lines as structured JSON. A second Gemini call judges every line on a rubric (punch, references, distinct voice, clean, French flavor). A failing opponent is regenerated twice, then the best scored candidate ships and the failure stays in the record. Every check appends a row to `evals/ledger.jsonl`. Lyria wrote eight tracks. `analyze-music.py` measures each one: beats, onsets, drops, breakdowns, energy per beat. Gemini 3.1 Flash Image painted the backdrops and portraits, Gradium voiced the lines.

The client. A scheduler starts the track on the Web Audio clock and stamps every key and touch onto the clock the player is hearing, output latency included, so what you see, hear and get judged on agree. The judge is plain beat grid math: 45 ms for Perfect, 90 ms for Great, 130 ms for Ok, tightened per level. The core turns judged results into aura, combo, score and events, and never imports the renderer. The director reads those events and the track analysis: it cuts the camera on downbeats, ramps slow motion into each drop and snaps back on it. The renderer (Three.js, drawn at 640x360 and blown up with nearest filtering) and the HUD only read events. Charts for levels 2 to 5 come from a seeded generator that places hits on real onsets, so a new track becomes a new level.

Nothing calls a model while you play. Every model output is a file in the bundle (`cast.json`, the tracks, the voices, the images), so the game is a static page that runs on a free host with the network off.

## Challenges

The beat tracker was off by about 250 ms. The hero track measured 129.2 BPM and is really 130.0, because the tracker rounds to whole analysis frames. Over 86 beats that drift puts the last notes a quarter second away from the music. We fold the onset envelope over a fine tempo scan and keep the sharpest pulse. Lyria also ignores the tempo we ask for: the club track was requested at 120 BPM and measures 129.2, the boss track was requested at 128 and measures 136, so we chart on what we measure and never on what we asked for. The other fight was AI grading AI. Gemini kept writing announcer lines over the 9 word limit, and the judge kept rejecting them with the count in the evidence, which is how the eval earned its place. Input latency differs between speakers, a laptop and Bluetooth headphones, so inputs are stamped on the clock the player hears and there is a calibration screen. Rights: nothing ships that was not generated by a partner model, released as CC0, or covered by Adobe's Mixamo terms. The reference track we listened to was measured and never copied.

## Accomplishments

Five levels, each about 40 s, with the first QTE inside 2 bars and no stretch longer than 2 bars without one, checked by a script rather than by eye. Eight Lyria tracks, 30 Gradium voice lines and 11 Nano Banana images generated for the cast. The evals ledger held 334 rows at 12:50: text checks 209 passed and 35 failed (each failure sent the line back for another try), music checks 35 passed and 5 failed, voice checks 30 passed, pacing checks 20 passed. A pure battle core, 84 unit tests across 8 files.

## What we learned

Charting on measured audio beats charting on the request. A rubric written before the content is cheap, and it catches what taste misses. Keeping the models out of the frame loop is what lets a static page on a free host run a game made by models and still work with the network off.
