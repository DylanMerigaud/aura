# Gen Z Canon 2026: Sounds, Moves, Phrases

Research pass for AURA's SFX, animation and copy passes. Scope: what a 16 year old recognizes on
TikTok as of September 2026, current window 2025 to September 2026, older references included only
where they are named explicitly as ANTI (do not use) material. Every fact carries a source URL.
Anything without a URL is marked (inferred), meaning it is general, widely known internet culture
knowledge rather than something pulled from a specific page in this research pass. Rights-free
reproduction methods use one of three forms only: re-voice the phrase with a TTS voice in the same
intonation, synthesize the effect with a stated recipe, or cannot, skip.

Two related documents already exist in this repo and are not repeated here: `aura-farming-spec.md`
(the aura farming meme, the boat dance, the street battle format, in full sourced depth) and
`music-reference.md` (the phonk and funk carioca sound palette measured off a reference track, and
AURA's own Lyria generated soundtrack). This document cross references both rather than duplicating
their sourcing.

## Section 2: Moves and gestures, 2025 to 2026 canon

Sixteen moves to use, keyed for a rigged character (hands, arms, head, torso, feet, timing, loop
behavior), followed by the ANTI list. Several of these are documented in full in
`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3 with Mixamo mapping; this list adds the moves outside the pure
boat dance lineage that section does not cover (the 67 hand gesture, the sigma stare, the Tung Tung
Sahur bat stance, the cooked collapse, the airball head shake, the aura battle face off) and
restates the boat dance and celebration moves briefly for completeness.

1. **The 67 hand gesture.** Hands: both palms face upward at roughly waist to chest height, moving
   in an alternating see-saw, one rising as the other falls, like weighing two options. Arms: bent
   at the elbow, forearms doing the motion, upper arms mostly still. Head and torso: still, eyes
   forward. Feet: planted. Timing: roughly half a second per up-down cycle, loops indefinitely
   while the "six, seven" line is being said. Created by high school basketball player Taylen
   Kinney, earliest documented instance December 16, 2024
   (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/67-hand-gesture); mainstream from the March 31, 2025 "67 kid"
   video (Maverick Trevillian) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-7). Rights: a hand gesture, not
   protected (see the rights note at the end of this section).

2. **Boat dance arm sweep.** Both arms rise from the sides to shoulder height and sweep forward
   together, wrists leading, torso still, 1.5 to 2 seconds per sweep, loops. From the Pacu Jalur
   togak luan role, viral globally from June 2025 (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 1, section 3
   move 1). Rights: not protected, a folk performance gesture with no single named choreographer
   claiming the specific edit derivative.

3. **Aura battle face off.** No fixed choreography: two competitors stand close, facing each other,
   holding still, composed expressions, occasionally breaking into a spontaneous pose or a stare
   down, while a crowd films and reacts; the crowd's laughter and reaction decide the "winner," not
   a judge (https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets).
   Hands: usually at the sides or in pockets. Head: level, unblinking where possible. Torso: still.
   Timing: holds for 2 to 5 seconds before either competitor breaks into a named move. Peaked:
   August 2026 spread across Latin America and Spain. This is the closest thing to a "summer 2026"
   move found in this pass. Rights: not protected, an improvised social format, not a fixed
   choreography.

4. **Sigma stare into mewing chin point.** Head: chin tucked down and slightly forward, jaw pressed
   with tongue to the roof of the mouth (the mewing pose), one hand's index finger pointing at own
   jawline. Eyes: narrowed, held on the opponent or camera. Torso and feet: still. Timing: a 2 to 3
   second hold, does not loop, resolves into another move. Documented together in the same Mexico
   City aura battle round as the Siuuu jump, won by 14 year old Carlos Irving Quintana
   (https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).
   Mewing itself traces to orthodontist Mike Mew's technique, popularized as internet culture from
   2019 (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/mewing). Rights: a facial pose, not protected.

5. **Siuuu jump.** A leap with both arms extended out and slightly back, one hand cupped near the
   ear on landing, feet together on the landing. Timing: under 1 second for the jump, then a held
   landing pose for 1 to 2 seconds. Cristiano Ronaldo's signature goal celebration, used as an
   explicit battle winning move in the same Mexico City round as entry 4 above (same source).
   Rights: a sports celebration gesture, not protected as choreography; Ronaldo's specific
   performance is his likeness, so keying the move generically (not modeling Ronaldo's face) avoids
   a likeness issue.

6. **Chin-up standing taunt.** Feet planted, chest out, chin raised, arms relaxed at the sides or
   loosely crossed, holds for 2 to 3 seconds with almost no movement (`aura-farming-spec.md`
   section 3, move 10). Rights: not protected, generic pose vocabulary.

7. **Chill guy hands-in-pockets saunter.** Same even, slow walking gait as a confident walk, but
   both hands disappear into the hip line as if in pockets, shoulders relaxed, chin level, reads
   from a distance as "not trying" (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3, move 8). The "Chill Guy"
   character (a brown dog in a jacket, hands in pockets) peaked as a standalone meme in November to
   December 2024 and later spawned an unrelated memecoin controversy; the character's specific
   illustrated design is somebody's copyrighted artwork, so use only the generic hands-in-pockets
   body language, never the dog character art itself (inferred, general knowledge of the character
   and its 2024 peak, not independently re-sourced with a fresh URL in this pass after repeated

   pockets, slow walk) is not protected; the specific illustrated dog character is somebody's
   artwork and should not be copied.

8. **The look back.** Torso keeps facing forward or keeps walking while the head and eyes turn back
   toward camera for 1 to 2 seconds, then return, a punctuation move, does not loop
   (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3, move 9). Rights: not protected, generic pose vocabulary.

9. **Griddy.** Feet: alternating heel taps, weight shifting side to side in place or while
   traveling. Arms: swing loosely in counter rhythm to the feet, not choreographed to a fixed
   count. Torso: slight bounce. Timing: a repeating cycle, roughly half a second per heel tap,
   loops as long as the celebration lasts. Created by Allen Davis, a high school football player
   from Harvey, Louisiana, around 2017 (https://historycooperative.org/who-invented-the-griddy),
   an NFL touchdown celebration staple since 2021 and a standing Fortnite emote. Rights: a dance
   step, not protected as choreography (see the rights note below); Davis is publicly credited as
   its originator but has not been shown to hold a copyright over it.

10. **Tung Tung Tung Sahur bat stance.** Feet: wide, stable stance. Hands: both gripping an
    imaginary bat or drumstick, held overhead or cocked at shoulder height. Torso: slight forward
    lean, ready posture. Head: still, facing forward. Timing: a held pose, 1 to 2 seconds, resolves
    into a swing or stays as an idle threat pose. Adapted from the Tung Tung Tung Sahur character's
    signature prop (a wooden bat or drumstick), Feb 2025 origin
    (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tung_Tung_Tung_Sahur), translated here into a physical pose for a
    rigged character rather than a 2D character reference. Rights: a generic ready stance, not
    protected; the specific character design is disputed IP and should not be copied.

11. **Wrist roll / snake arms.** One arm undulates shoulder to fingertip in a traveling wave, the
    other stays low, head tilts slightly to follow the moving hand, 2 to 3 seconds, loops
    (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3, move 3). Rights: not protected.

12. **Palm push.** Both palms press outward from the chest and release slowly, elbows close to the
    body, about 1 second out and 1 second back, loops 2 to 3 times (`aura-farming-spec.md`
    section 3, move 2). Rights: not protected.

13. **Point at camera.** Arm extends straight toward the lens, index finger out, held 1 second
    (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3, move 12). Rights: not protected.

14. **Sunglasses adjust.** One hand rises to push sunglasses up the nose bridge or down to reveal
    the eyes, held half a second, then drops, tied to the original boat kid's signature look
    (`aura-farming-spec.md` section 3, move 11 and section 1). Rights: not protected.

15. **The cooked collapse.** Shoulders drop suddenly, knees bend inward slightly, head hangs
    forward, arms go loose at the sides. Timing: under 1 second to collapse into the pose, held for
    1 to 2 seconds, does not loop, a defeat punctuation reflecting "you're cooked" (section 1,
    entry 15). Reference: general defeat body language, formalized here to match the sound.
    Rights: not protected, generic pose vocabulary (inferred pairing of a documented phrase to a
    plausible physical read, the specific pose itself is not independently sourced to a named
    viral clip in this pass).

16. **The airball head shake.** Head: a slow side to side shake, 2 to 3 repetitions over about 2
    seconds. One hand: rises to the forehead, a facepalm variant, held through the shake. Torso:
    still. Reflects "holy airball" (section 1, entry 8), peaked May 2025
    (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/holy-airball). Rights: not protected, generic disappointment
    body language paired here with a sourced phrase.

### Rights note on gestures generally

A short hand gesture or dance step, by itself, is not protected choreography in the way a full,
staged, multi-step dance routine can be. The clearest real world illustration is the 2018 to 2019
dispute in which rapper 2 Milly and actor Alfonso Ribeiro separately pursued claims against Epic
Games over Fortnite's paid emotes based on the Milly Rock and the Carlton dance; neither action
produced a ruling that a short dance move is protectable, and both were dropped or ran into
copyright registration limits before reaching that question (inferred, general knowledge of this
dispute, not independently re-sourced with a fresh URL in this pass). The practical rule for AURA:
build the animation library from named, individual gestures (as this section does), never by
lifting one continuous, staged choreography wholesale from a specific troupe or production.

## Section 4: The world these pieces unify into

What ties every sound, move and phrase above into one coherent world is that AURA is not staged as
a fighting game with a neutral camera, it is staged as a phone recording of a live TikTok moment: a
portrait frame, a live viewer count ticking in a corner, a scroll of hearts and comments (some
reading "+2,500 aura" or "holy airball" in real time as the crowd scores what it is watching), a
faint camera shake and occasional refocus hunt as if a real hand is holding the phone, and a
timestamp or "LIVE" badge burned into the frame the way a battle crowd's own footage would carry
one. Every sound in section 1 already exists to be dropped into exactly this kind of frame; every
move in section 2 already exists to be judged, unscored, by exactly this kind of crowd, per the one
law documented in `aura-farming-spec.md` section 2 (the harder someone visibly tries to farm aura,
the more likely the room decides they have lost it). The game's UI chrome should look like the
overlay a spectator's own phone would already be showing them at a real street battle, not like a
traditional game HUD. Representative live examples of this grammar already in the wild: the
Euronews video coverage of an actual Mexico City street aura battle
(https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets),
CapCut's own "Aura Farming Edit" template, which packages the slow motion drop, the glow effect and
the aura points overlay into one reusable kit
(https://www.capcut.com/explore/aura-farming-edit), and the #holyairball tag page, whose feed of
short clips is the "miss" half of the same viewing grammar
(https://www.tiktok.com/tag/holyairball).
