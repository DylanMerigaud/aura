# Aura Farming: Research Spec for AURA

Research pass on the aura farming meme and its "competitions," compressed into eval rubrics for
the game's animation, character, and edit passes. Facts carry a source URL. Anything without a
URL is marked (inferred).

## 1. What it is

"Aura farming" first circulated as slang in 2024, well before the dance that now defines it. The
earliest documented use is a TikTok clip by @h.chua_212 posted January 28, 2024, captioned "Aura
Farming," set to "TORE UP" by Don Toliver
(https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/aura-farming). The phrase crossed into general internet slang on
X on September 13, 2024, when @scubaryan_ used it to describe rapper Duke Dennis being accused of
performatively "aura farming" while eating grapes
(https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/2997018-aura-farming). Through late 2024 the term spread mainly
in anime fan communities, applied jokingly to characters (Piccolo, Sasuke) who pose or stall for
dramatic effect before acting (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/aura-farming).

The meme's defining image arrived separately. Pacu Jalur is a centuries-old boat racing festival
in Kuantan Singingi (Kuansing), Riau province, Sumatra: teams row wooden boats up to 40 meters
long, and a child called the "togak luan" (togak, upright, luan, bow of the boat) stands at the
bow to energize the rowers and signal when the boat is leading
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_farming,
https://www.kompas.id/artikel/en-tarian-bocah-dalam-tradisi-pacu-jalur-mendunia-kenapa-bisa-terjadi).
On the togak luan role: "if the tempo shifts by even a fraction of a second, the entire boat is
thrown into chaos" (same Kompas.id source), and a togak luan whose boat falls behind can jump into
the water to lighten the load (same source).

An 11-year-old named Rayyan Arkan Dikha performed as togak luan at the Teluk Kuantan festival. The
original video was posted in January 2025 by TikTok user @lensa.rams
(https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-the-aura-farming-indonesian-boat-racing-kid-the-viral-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-memes-explained),
and it did not go viral until June 2025 when Instagram Reels accounts began re-cutting it to
music (https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/2997018-aura-farming). By early July 2025 the clip had
spawned roughly 200,000 copycat videos under #aurafarming on TikTok alone
(https://www.scmp.com/postmag/culture/article/3327219/sumatran-longboat-festival-spawned-global-obsession-aura-farming).
Dikha wore a black Teluk Belanga outfit, a Malay Riau headcloth, and sunglasses, and has said the
dance was his own invention: "No-one taught me, it was just spontaneous"
(https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-08-15/aura-farming-boat-kid-draws-global-attention-and-online-scam/105641912).
He had trained as togak luan since age 8 or 9, practicing three times a week with his father (same
ABC source). The clip that carried the trend globally was set to "Young Black & Rich" by Melly
Mike (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Black_%26_Rich,
https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-the-aura-farming-indonesian-boat-racing-kid-the-viral-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-memes-explained).
Phonk and funk carioca (Brazilian funk, montagem) tracks are named as the genres that carried the
edit trend further once it left the original clip
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_farming), which lines up directly with AURA's own funk
montagem phonk soundtrack.

Replication moved from meme pages into sport and celebrity within days. Paris Saint-Germain
posted their own version captioned "His aura made it all the way to Paris"
(https://www.abc.net.au/world/asia/indonesian-11-year-old-aura-farmer-becomes-internet-sensation-with-viral-boat-dance-1.500200119
via https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/indonesian-11-year-old-aura-farmer-becomes-internet-sensation-with-viral-boat-dance-1.500200119).
AC Milan's mascot recreated it, and Marc Marquez used the dance as his podium celebration after
winning the 2025 German MotoGP on a Ducati (same Gulf News source). Travis Kelce's version passed
14 million views, and Alex Albon, Diego Luna, KSI, Steve Aoki, Wiz Khalifa, Chloe Kelly (during
England's Euro 2025 victory parade), and sailors from the US, Singaporean, and Polish navies all
did their own take (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_farming). Dikha was later named a Cultural
Ambassador of Riau and met government ministers
(https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/2997018-aura-farming). By August 2025 scam accounts were
impersonating him, demanding over $30,000 for fake collaborations and Dubai tour promotions
(https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-08-15/aura-farming-boat-kid-draws-global-attention-and-online-scam/105641912).

From there, "aura farming" became the umbrella name for live, in-person "aura battles": teenagers
squaring off in public to out-charisma each other. These are documented across Mexico, Argentina,
Brazil, Peru, Bolivia, Spain, Honduras, Ecuador, and Venezuela starting around August 2026
(https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets,
https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-the-aura-farming-indonesian-boat-racing-kid-the-viral-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-memes-explained).
Section 2 covers that format in detail.

Two threads worth keeping distinct for the game's story: (1) the 2024 slang meaning of "aura
farming" (trying too hard to look cool, usually said mockingly), and (2) the 2025 to 2026 physical
meme built on Dikha's dance and its live-battle descendants. AURA's animation criteria in section
5 draw on thread 2, since that is the "best moves" material Dylan asked for.

## 2. The competitions

There is no single official aura farming competition format. Three documented shapes exist, all
consistent with each other:

**One-on-one street battles (Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Peru, Bolivia, Spain, Honduras, Ecuador,
Venezuela, 2026).** Two competitors, mostly aged 14 to 20, face off in a public square (an early
Mexico City edition ran at the Palacio de Bellas Artes), surrounded by a crowd that films on
phones while music resembling a video game soundtrack plays
(https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets).
There is no fixed choreography: a battle can look like a dance-off, a meme-reenactment contest, or
two people simply staring each other down with theatrical composure
(https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-the-aura-farming-indonesian-boat-racing-kid-the-viral-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-memes-explained).
No judges or point sheet: the crowd's laughter and reaction decide the winner. Organizer Aldhir
Gonzalez (22, Mexico City): "The one who looks better, shows off more, gets more laughs and has
more confidence wins" (https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets,
near-identical quote also at
https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).
Prizes are informal: 3,000 Mexican pesos (about $177) in one Mexico City edition, "infinite aura"
(bragging rights, no cash) in an Argentina edition
(https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets).
A named example: 14-year-old Carlos Irving Quintana won his round recreating Cristiano Ronaldo's
"Siuuu" celebration and the "mewing" jawline pose, against an opponent countering with the "6-7"
hand gesture (https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).

**"Aura points" as a scoring vocabulary (TikTok-wide, 2024 onward, not tied to a live event).**
Independent of the street battles, TikTok users score everyday clips in the comments by adding or
subtracting "aura points": "rocked a new outfit at the party, plus 2,500 aura points" versus
"spilled my drink in front of everyone, minus 1,000 aura points"
(https://www.plannthat.com/social-media-glossary/aura-points/,
https://www.bustle.com/wellness/aura-points-tiktok). There is no official point total or ruleset;
it is audience vibes turned into a fake scoreboard (same sources). This is the origin of any
"+1000 aura" or "-500 aura" overlay text a game HUD might reference.

**School and talent-show reenactments.** Individual kids perform the boat-kid dance or a
freestyle "aura" routine at school talent shows; documented example: "RizzKidKarter performs Aura
Farm at school talent show" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YzRHQkA5NR4). These are solo
performances judged the same way any talent-show act is (audience response), not a scored
aura-specific format.

**The one law that holds across every format**, stated explicitly by a Know Your Meme writer
covering the street battles: "the harder someone visibly tries to farm aura, the more likely
people are to decide they have lost it"
(https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/who-is-the-aura-farming-indonesian-boat-racing-kid-the-viral-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-memes-explained).
Composure reads as aura; visible effort reads as its opposite. This paradox is the single most
important design constraint for AURA's animation and face work (see section 5).

## 3. The canon of moves

Twelve to twenty named moves, grouped by lineage. Mixamo animation names are given where a search
turned up that exact name attached to Mixamo (confirmed); where no exact match was found, the
closest real Mixamo catalog entry is proposed and marked (inferred).

### (a) Boat dance vocabulary

1. **Slow symmetrical arm sweep.** Both arms rise from the sides to shoulder height and sweep
   forward together, wrists leading, torso still. About 1.5 to 2 seconds per sweep, loops.
   Mixamo: **Wave Hip Hop Dance** (confirmed name, https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/wave-hip-hop-dance-mixamo-213a18f2c83544e99b0663a3ae9741b0).
2. **Palm push.** Both palms press outward from the chest and release slowly, elbows staying
   close to the body. About 1 second out, 1 second back, loops 2 to 3 times.
   Mixamo: **Arm Stretching** (confirmed catalog name, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811,
   mapping to this specific gesture is inferred).
3. **Wrist roll / snake arms.** One arm undulates shoulder to fingertip in a traveling wave,
   the other stays low; head tilts slightly to follow the moving hand. 2 to 3 seconds, loops.
   Mixamo: **Snake Hip Hop Dance** (confirmed name,
   https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/snake-hip-hop-dance-be2fa6a946a449d29a6ace0eebf91dbc).
4. **Reach and pull.** One arm extends fully toward the crowd, palm up, then draws back sharply
   to the chest as if pulling in energy; weight shifts back on the pull. About 1 second per
   cycle, does not loop, resolves into a still pose.
   Mixamo: **House Dancing** (confirmed catalog name, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811,
   mapping is inferred).
5. **Still torso sway.** Hips and shoulders sway side to side on a slow count while the arms
   stay low and relaxed; the only moving parts below the neck are hips and shoulders. Loops
   indefinitely, this is the idle state between named moves.
   Mixamo: **Idle** (confirmed generic name; a dance-flavored variant such as **Jazz Dancing**
   is a plausible substitute, both inferred as the sway match).

### (b) Walk and pose vocabulary

6. **Slow confident walk.** Even stride, shoulders back, no arm swing past hip height, chin
   level. Traversal move, does not loop in place.
   Mixamo: **Strut Walking** (confirmed name, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811).
7. **Catwalk strut.** Exaggerated hip-led stride with a slight knee lift, used for entrances.
   Mixamo: **Catwalk Walk Forward HighKnees** (confirmed exact name,
   https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/catwalk-walk-from-mixamo-6ae275278285465bb90e6df1338f0384,
   https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811).
8. **Hands-in-pockets saunter.** Same slow walk as move 6 but arms disappear at the hip line;
   read from a distance as "not trying." Mixamo: **Walking** with hands lowered
   (inferred, no dedicated pockets animation found).
9. **Over-the-shoulder look back.** Torso keeps walking or facing forward while the head and
   eyes turn back toward camera for 1 to 2 seconds, then return. Does not loop, a punctuation
   move. Mixamo: **Looking Around** (confirmed name,
   https://www.sansar.com/store/listings/aa16577f-ec0d-4c18-aec0-bef2f6d2c48e/standing-idle-animation---look-around-scared-standing-idle---mixamo,
   mapping to the "aura" glance-back is inferred since Mixamo's version is a generic idle look).
10. **Chin-up stare / standing taunt.** Feet planted, chest out, chin raised, holds for 2 to 3
    seconds with almost no movement. Mixamo: **Taunt** (confirmed catalog name,
    https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/taunt-0d9dd4efec9b4add97954e6d3830a243).
11. **Sunglasses adjust.** One hand rises to push sunglasses up the nose bridge or down to
    reveal the eyes, held half a second, then drops. No confirmed dedicated Mixamo animation
    (inferred, would need a short custom hand-to-face blend).
12. **Point at camera.** Arm extends straight toward the lens, index finger out, held 1 second.
    No confirmed dedicated Mixamo animation (inferred, closest generic base is **Taunt** or a
    custom gesture blend).
13. **Shoulder brush / collar pop.** One hand brushes the opposite shoulder or flicks a collar,
    quick, under a second, usually paired with move 10. No confirmed Mixamo name (inferred).

### (c) Celebration vocabulary migrated in

14. **The Siuuu jump.** A leap with both arms extended out and slightly back, one hand cupped
    near the ear on landing; Cristiano Ronaldo's signature goal celebration, explicitly used as
    a battle-winning move by 14-year-old Carlos Irving Quintana
    (https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).
    Mixamo: **Jumping Dance** (confirmed catalog name,
    https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811, mapping to the specific
    Siuuu shape is inferred).
15. **Chest-out victory pose.** Arms raised in a V or fists at the chest, held for a beat, used
    as a battle finisher. Mixamo: **Victory** (a long-standing Mixamo catalog name, not
    independently confirmed by URL in this research pass, marked inferred).
16. **Crowd-cheering response.** Both arms pumping overhead, small forward-back bounce, used by
    the audience or a winning competitor. Mixamo: **Cheering** (the seated variant
    **Cheering While Sitting** is confirmed, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3103817811;
    a standing equivalent is inferred to exist in the same family).
17. **Mewing jawline point.** Chin tucked, tongue-to-palate jaw pose, one finger pointing at own
    jawline; used alongside the Siuuu jump in the same Mexico City round
    (https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).
    No Mixamo equivalent found (inferred, face-only, not a body animation).

### (d) Anti moves that lose aura (never let the winning side do these)

18. **The stumble.** A missed step, arms flail briefly for balance, immediately caught. Under a
    second. Mixamo: **Stumble Backwards** (confirmed name,
    https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/stumble-backwards-1-3bb712270fd7437094a166f429e10710).
19. **The smile break.** A held serious pose visibly cracks into a grin or laugh. This is a
    face-only failure, not a skeletal animation; best implemented as a blendshape trigger
    layered on top of any pose (inferred, no Mixamo body animation applies).
20. **The approval-seeking glance.** Mid-pose, the head turns to check the crowd's reaction and
    the shoulders round forward, breaking the still upper body of moves 1 to 10. Mixamo:
    **Looking Around** reused with a hunched, uncertain variant rather than the confident
    version in move 9 (inferred).

This directly encodes the rule from section 2: "the harder someone visibly tries to farm aura,
the more likely people are to decide they have lost it." Moves 18 through 20 are the mechanical
opposite of moves 1 through 13: broken stillness, broken symmetry, or a face that seeks approval
instead of ignoring the crowd.

## 4. The edit grammar

Aura farming edits share a small, repeated visual and audio kit, whether hand-cut on CapCut or
built from a template:

- **Slow motion on the payoff, not the buildup.** CapCut's own "Aura Farming Edit" template is
  built around a "velocity slowmo" beat drop, i.e. real-time footage that suddenly drops to slow
  motion at the musical hit (https://www.capcut.com/explore/aura-farming-edit).
- **Glow or "aura" halo effect.** A glowing outline or energy field rendered around the subject,
  frequently color-shifted to signal a power change, sometimes combined with a freeze frame for
  the reveal (https://www.capcut.com/explore/aura-farming-edit; the effect is described generally
  as "a glowing halo or energetic field, often used in anime edits, power-up scenes, and gaming
  content").
- **"Aura points" text overlays.** Floating "+2,500 aura" or "-1,000 aura" captions layered over
  a clip, borrowed directly from the comment-section scoring habit in section 2
  (https://www.plannthat.com/social-media-glossary/aura-points/,
  https://www.bustle.com/wellness/aura-points-tiktok).
- **Phonk and funk carioca (montagem) audio beds**, named specifically as the soundtrack that
  carried aura farming edits past the original clip
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_farming). This is the same genre family as AURA's own
  soundtrack.
- **Crowd as camera.** In the live battle format, the "edit" is literally shot on spectator
  phones surrounding the performer, meaning every clip already has a built-in reaction-shot ring
  (https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/08/22/what-is-aura-farming-tiktoks-strange-viral-battles-hit-the-streets).
- **Sunglasses and stillness as the visual anchor.** Both the original boat-kid clip and its
  replications lean on sunglasses plus a near-static upper body as the one constant image, even
  as the surrounding effects change (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-08-15/aura-farming-boat-kid-draws-global-attention-and-online-scam/105641912).

No independently sourced usage of "unfathomable aura" as a fixed caption format was found in this
pass; it is a plausible community caption in the same register as "+1000 aura" but is not
confirmed with a URL and should be treated as (inferred) if used.

## 5. The essence, as eval rubrics

### (a) ANIMATION rubric (score each move 1 to 5)

- **STILLNESS.** 5: everything below the neck that is not part of the named move is dead still,
  zero fidget, zero micro-adjustment for the full hold.
- **CONTROL.** 5: every limb moves at one constant, slow velocity with no acceleration spikes,
  and left and right sides mirror each other when the move calls for symmetry.
- **LANDING ON THE BEAT.** 5: the pose fully resolves (zero residual velocity) within one frame
  of the downbeat, not before, not after.
- **FACE.** 5: neutral or faintly bored expression throughout, no smile, no eyebrow raise, no
  glance toward camera or crowd for reaction.
- **READABILITY.** 5: a person with no context names the move correctly from a single still
  frame or a 1-second clip with no audio.
- **CANON.** 5: the move is on the list in section 3 verbatim, or a documented variant of one; 1:
  it is generic dance filler invented for the game with no traceable lineage.

### (b) CHARACTER rubric (score the opponent 1 to 5)

- 5: the opponent is legible as one specific aura archetype within 2 seconds of appearing (the
  boat dancer, the football celebrator, the mewer, the street-battle stare-down kid), signaled by
  a single costume or prop read, not a paragraph of design.
- 3: the opponent reads as "aura-trend adjacent" but the specific archetype takes more than one
  full move to identify.
- 1: the opponent could be swapped into a generic fighting game with no loss of meaning.

### (c) LEVEL or EDIT rubric (mechanical)

- **Time to first move**: under 3 seconds from level start, or it is dead time.
- **Longest gap with no QTE, no camera event, and no beat-synced cut**: under 4 seconds; any
  longer gap is a fail.
- **One signature moment per level**: exactly one moment (a freeze, a crowd reaction cut, a
  slow-motion drop) is visually distinct from every other beat in that level; zero such moments
  or three or more competing "biggest" moments both fail this axis.
- **Drop alignment**: the level's climax (final QTE or combo) resolves within 1 beat of the
  track's drop, measured in the track's own BPM grid.
- **Crowd reaction cuts**: at least one cut to the ring of onlookers per 15 seconds of gameplay,
  matching the "crowd as camera" grammar in section 4.

### (d) Ten instant-cringe conditions (each one is a kill, binary, no partial credit)

1. The character smiles or laughs while a serious pose is being held (move 19, section 3).
2. The character's head turns toward the camera or crowd mid-pose in a way that reads as
   checking for approval rather than performing to them (move 20).
3. A symmetrical move (arm sweep, palm push) is visibly asymmetric between left and right sides.
4. A pose resolves more than one beat off the downbeat.
5. Any limb overshoots and visibly corrects (a wobble) during a hold.
6. The character trips or stumbles (move 18) while winning, not losing, a QTE sequence.
7. Two or more unrelated canon moves play back to back with no held beat between them (reads as
   random dancing, not composed aura).
8. A move plays faster than its documented pace in section 3 (fast reads as trying hard, which
   the section 2 quote names as the exact way to lose).
9. The camera cuts away from a move before it resolves.
10. The same move repeats twice in a row with no variation in framing, camera angle, or crowd
    reaction between the two plays.

## 6. Ranked top 8 for a first build

Mapped to the four arrow QTEs, the mash-and-release, the hold (freeze), and the combo sequence.
Ranking logic: canon-defining moves first, then pose-vocabulary moves, then the moves that best
fit each QTE's native input shape, finisher last.

1. **Arrow QTE 1, over-the-shoulder look back.** The single most repeated punctuation gesture in
   both the original clip and its replications. Mixamo: **Looking Around** (confirmed).
2. **Arrow QTE 2, slow symmetrical arm sweep.** The core boat-dance move, most visually distinct
   from generic dance. Mixamo: **Wave Hip Hop Dance** (confirmed).
3. **Arrow QTE 3, wrist roll / snake arms.** Second half of the boat-dance pair, reads well at a
   distance because of the traveling wave shape. Mixamo: **Snake Hip Hop Dance** (confirmed).
4. **Arrow QTE 4, chin-up stare / standing taunt.** The pose vocabulary's clearest single-frame
   read. Mixamo: **Taunt** (confirmed).
5. **Mash-and-release ("the 69"), hip shimmy build into a held pose.** A rapid repeated motion
   that must resolve into stillness on release, matching the mash-then-hold input shape. Mixamo:
   **Snake Hip Hop Dance** or **Twerk** for the mash phase, resolving into **Taunt** on release
   (confirmed names, this specific pairing to the input type is a design choice, not a sourced
   fact).
6. **Hold (freeze), breakdance freeze.** The input type and the move share a name; a hold that
   does not resolve into stillness is a wasted mechanic. Mixamo: **Breakdance Freeze** (this
   exact name is standard Mixamo naming for the move but was not independently confirmed by URL
   in this pass; treat as inferred until checked against a logged-in Mixamo session).
7. **Combo sequence, Siuuu jump into chest-out victory pose.** The one finisher documented as an
   actual battle-winning combo in a real aura battle
   (https://www.geo.tv/latest/679154-aura-farming-explained-how-viral-internet-memes-became-street-competition).
   Mixamo: **Jumping Dance** into **Victory** (Jumping Dance confirmed, Victory inferred).
8. **Bonus, catwalk strut for level entrance or exit.** Not a QTE move, an entrance or transition
   beat that sets the "not trying" tone before the first QTE. Mixamo: **Catwalk Walk Forward
   HighKnees** (confirmed).
